Contributing

Thank you for helping build a responsible public community around children’s digital well-being.

Before you contribute

* Protect children’s privacy at all times.
* Share only information you have the right to disclose.
* Keep proposals public, non-confidential, and appropriate for an open repository.
* Read the Public/Private IP Boundary.
* Read the Public Technical Boundary.
* Follow the Community Code of Conduct.

Good contributions

We welcome:

* Evidence-based child-safety resources
* Plain-language educational material for families
* Accessibility recommendations
* Age-appropriate design principles
* Public policy or standards references
* Clearly defined non-confidential feature proposals
* Pilot-program concepts
* Feedback from parents, educators, youth advocates, and safety professionals

Never include

* Personally identifying information about a child
* Private screenshots, conversations, case records, or allegations
* Credentials, secrets, internal system details, or vulnerability instructions
* Confidential business information
* Proprietary code or architecture
* Material copied without permission

How to submit an idea

1. Search existing issues to avoid duplicates.
2. Select the closest issue template.
3. Describe the problem before proposing a solution.
4. Identify who benefits.
5. Explain safety, privacy, accessibility, and misuse concerns.
6. Include reliable public sources where appropriate.

Review process

Maintainers may:

* Ask for clarification or supporting evidence
* Edit titles or labels for organization
* Move a discussion to a more appropriate location
* Remove sensitive or identifying information
* Close submissions that are unsafe, off-topic, duplicative, confidential, or inconsistent with project boundaries

Submission does not guarantee adoption, partnership, compensation, or incorporation into any product.

Contributions and rights

By submitting public text, ideas, or materials, you confirm that you have the right to submit them and permit the project to display, discuss, organize, and evaluate them within this public repository. Do not submit anything requiring confidentiality or a separate license agreement.

For formal partnerships, research collaborations, commercial proposals, or protected disclosures, use an approved private business channel rather than a public GitHub issue.
