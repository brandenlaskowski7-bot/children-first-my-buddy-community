Trademark and Brand Policy

Project names, program names, product names, logos, slogans, visual identities, account names, and related branding may be protected even when displayed publicly.

Permitted references

You may use plain-text project names to truthfully identify or discuss the project, provided you do not suggest sponsorship, endorsement, certification, partnership, ownership, or official status.

Permission required

Written permission is required to:

* Use a project name or logo in a product, service, company, domain, application, event, fundraising campaign, publication title, social-media account, or merchandise
* Create a confusingly similar name, logo, or visual identity
* Claim to be an official chapter, partner, representative, reseller, developer, trainer, or certification provider
* Use project branding in advertising or commercial promotion
* Modify or combine project branding with another organization’s branding

Good-faith community references

Accurate statements such as “submitted feedback to the Children First / My Buddy public repository” may be acceptable when they are factual, non-commercial, and do not imply endorsement.

The owner may request correction or removal of uses that create confusion, misrepresent affiliation, endanger children, undermine trust, or interfere with protected rights.
